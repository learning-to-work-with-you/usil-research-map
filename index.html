User-Specific Interaction Learning

A living map of how agents learn to work with you

This map annotates the research behind User-Specific Interaction Learning (USIL): work on learning about individual users, and work on when agents should ask, act, confirm, or defer. Each paper is labeled by what it learns, whether it learns from one person's evidence, and which interaction decisions it affects.

Living map

Built together, and never finished. This is not a fixed review. We keep finding papers, reading them in full, and correcting labels, and each version is a snapshot of where the field stands. Researchers in agent learning, HCI, personalization, and robotics shape it by suggesting papers, corrections, and open questions.

How to read this map

What a label means

Each paper is labeled by what it reports, not by what its method could support.

  • YesReported, with evidence in the paper.
  • PartialOnly in part, e.g. for one kind of decision or within a single session.
  • NoThe paper clearly does not.
  • Not statedThe paper was read and does not say. This is not the same as No.

Where the labels come from

  • Full textLabeled by a language model from the whole paper; every supporting quote is checked against the PDF.
  • By handLabeled by the map's contributors.
  • Not yet annotatedListed in Browse, but left out of the charts until it is labeled.
